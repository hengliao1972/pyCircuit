\section{Legacy Notice}
\label{sec:legacy_corpus}
This appendix documents the TileOp microcode source corpus as it was defined in an earlier revision of this manual. The binary encoding described in this appendix (the 32-bit WO / 32-bit WM split with the field widths documented below) is a v0 encoding that predates the current revision.

\textbf{This appendix is informational only.} The authoritative encoding for this revision is Chapter~\ref{chap:encodings}, which defines the current 64-bit WO/WM micro-instruction format (WO at bits \texttt{63:32}, WM at bits \texttt{31:0}) with the field widths given in Section~\ref{sec:micro_instruction_encoding}. The v0 encoding in this appendix MUST NOT be used as the reference for new implementations; it is retained for historical context and for tooling that was built against the older format.

Implementers who need to understand the current PTO micro-instruction encoding should begin with Section~\ref{sec:micro_instruction_encoding} and the per-instruction microcode site tables in the tile instruction reference pages.

This appendix provides the TileOp microcode source corpus used by the encoding tables. It records field definitions, opcode body sketches, and corpus-level reference material for implementers.

\section{Two-Word 64-bit Microinstruction Format (v0 Legacy)}

Each TileOp microinstruction is encoded as a pair of 32-bit RISC words. WO (Word Operation) encodes the PTO TileOp and its operands. WM (Word Metadata) encodes microcode sequencing and control.

\textbf{Note: this section describes the v0 encoding.} In the current revision, the micro-instruction format is a single 64-bit word with WO at bits \texttt{63:32} and WM at bits \texttt{31:0} (see Section~\ref{sec:micro_instruction_encoding}). The field widths and semantic assignments below differ from the current format.

\subsection{Word Operation (WO) --- 32 bits}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cllr}
\toprule
Bits & Field & Description & Bits \\
\midrule
31:25 & \texttt{WO\_OPCODE} & PTO TileOp opcode index (0--69) & 7 \\
24 & \texttt{WO\_IS\_EXT} & 0=PTO-BC v0, 1=golden/vendor extension & 1 \\
23:21 & \texttt{WO\_FLAGS} & Op kind: 000=mem, 001=compute, 010=compute+var, 011=composite & 3 \\
20:14 & \texttt{WO\_SEQ} & Zero-based microinstruction sequence within TileOp entry (max 87) & 7 \\
13:12 & \texttt{WO\_OP\_KIND} & 00=tile reg, 01=imm addr, 10=scalar, 11=imm value & 2 \\
11:8 & \texttt{WO\_OP\_A} & Operand A: register index or immediate value & 4 \\
7:4 & \texttt{WO\_OP\_B} & Operand B: register index or immediate value & 4 \\
3:0 & \texttt{WO\_OP\_C} & Operand C: register index or immediate value & 4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Total: $7+1+3+7+2+4+4+4 = 32$ bits.

The PTO opcode index is the flat index into the unified opcode table (see Section~\ref{sec:opcode_table}). Opcode indices 0--63 correspond to PTO-BC v0; 64--69 correspond to golden/vendor extensions.

WO encodes the operation itself. Operands A, B, C are register specifiers or immediate values depending on WO\_OP\_KIND. For memory operations (WO\_FLAGS=000), A is the address register, B is the data register. For compute operations (WO\_FLAGS=001), A and B are source tile registers, C is the destination tile register.

\subsection{Word Metadata (WM) --- 32 bits}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cllr}
\toprule
Bits & Field & Description & Bits \\
\midrule
31:25 & \texttt{WM\_ENTRY\_ID} & Microcode entry ID: index into TileOp lookup table (0--69) & 7 \\
24:23 & \texttt{WM\_OP\_COUNT} & Number of operands: 01=1, 10=2, 11=3, 00=4 & 2 \\
22:21 & \texttt{WM\_RES\_TYPE} & Result tile type: 00=vec, 01=scalar, 10=accum, 11=reserved & 2 \\
20:18 & \texttt{WM\_SRC\_TYPE\_A} & Source A tile type & 2 \\
17:15 & \texttt{WM\_SRC\_TYPE\_B} & Source B tile type & 2 \\
14:12 & \texttt{WM\_SRC\_TYPE\_C} & Source C tile type & 2 \\
11:9 & \texttt{WM\_VARIANT} & PTO-BC variant (always 0 in corpus; reserved) & 3 \\
8:0 & \texttt{WM\_SPARE} & Reserved for future use & 9 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Total: $7+2+2+2+2+2+3+9 = 29$ bits. Plus 3 spare bits in WO (bits 20:18 unused when WO\_OP\_KIND=00). Combined total: 64 bits.

WM provides microcode sequencing metadata. WM\_ENTRY\_ID indexes the TileOp microcode lookup table to locate the base address of this TileOp's microinstruction sequence. WM\_OP\_COUNT is the effective bytecode operand count. Tile types (RES\_TYPE, SRC\_TYPE\_*) encode the expected tile storage class: vec=UB tile buffer, scalar=SPR/GPR, accum=accumulator tile.

\subsection{Decoding Algorithm}

For each 64-bit microinstruction pair (WO, WM):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Decode WO: extract WO\_OPCODE + WO\_IS\_EXT $\rightarrow$ PTO TileOp opcode.
  \item Use WM\_ENTRY\_ID to look up the TileOp microcode entry base address.
  \item Use WO\_SEQ to index into the microinstruction sequence starting at that base.
  \item Check WO\_FLAGS to determine operation kind (memory/compute/composite).
  \item Extract operands from WO\_OP\_A, WO\_OP\_B, WO\_OP\_C per WO\_OP\_KIND.
  \item Validate WM\_OP\_COUNT against the TileOp's expected arity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{TSTORE Opcode Reference}

The \texttt{TSTORE} opcode (PTO-BC 0x1065, Opcode Index 25, WO\_OPCODE=25) is the primitive store operation. Its WO encoding follows the same format as tload, with WO\_OP\_A=addr register, WO\_OP\_B=data register, WO\_OP\_C=unused.

WO\_SEQ & varies & Per-sequence position \\
WO\_OP\_KIND & 00 & Tile register operands \\
WO\_OP\_A & addr reg (0--15) & Tile buffer address register \\
WO\_OP\_B & data reg (0--15) & Data source tile register \\
WO\_OP\_C & 0 & Unused for tstore \\
